Works with any ATS, recruiting CRM or tracker, any calendar, any email, and any meeting platform. Paste everything under the line into your LLM once. It will wait. When you want a brief, send "Run Flight Status for [REQ]" along with the variables below (or fill them in before pasting).

| [JOB LEVEL] | Which level applies (default levels: IC, People Leader, Executive). Your system of record may not carry a level, so supply it. |

# RUN CONDITION (read first)

Do NOT build anything when you receive this prompt. Reply only with: "Flight Status is loaded. Tell me which req to run." Then wait.

Build a brief ONLY when I explicitly ask in a message, e.g. "Run Flight Status for [req]". Never run on a schedule, timer, new file or calendar event, or on your own initiative. One request = one req, delivered as two files: the full brief and a one-page summary (see SHORT BRIEF). If I say "full only" or "summary only", build just that one. For a refresh, wait for me to ask again. If the req, job level or system of record is missing, ask for it (counts toward the 3-question limit below) instead of guessing.

# TASK

Build "Flight Status": a self-contained HTML brief that tells the story of ONE requisition, [REQ], from kickoff to its current state. Think of each search as a flight. The brief answers: where are we, are we on schedule, and what is holding us up?

Readers: [READERS]. Default audience is the hiring team; see Privacy for wider sharing. If [READERS] go beyond the hiring team (for example a VP or leadership) and I have not said whether to use "share-safe", ask once (counts toward the 3-question limit); if I cannot be asked, use share-safe.

Sections, in this order:
1. **Header**: req title and flight state in the hero (In Flight / Landed / On Hold / Canceled); department, hiring manager, priority, open date, target close, job level and "as of" date in the sidebar facts. Closed as Filled = "Landed": present final results. Closed without a hire = "Canceled": show the close reason and the final funnel.
2. **Headline**: plain-language narrative of the search, 3 sentences or fewer and 60 words or fewer: where the search stands against its Time to Fill target, the single biggest constraint, and what is next. Do not recite funnel counts or restate every metric; the tiles and runway carry those. The count of metrics On Time / At Risk / Delayed / No Data appears only as the sidebar status dots. Write it LAST, after every metric, funnel and velocity number is computed, using only those values. When it names the slowest stage, use the metric 5 rule. Self-check: every number and stage named must match a tile or chart on the page; if not, rewrite.
3. **Key Metrics**: the five metrics (definitions in Context). Time to Fill is the hero numeral in the header; the other four are tiles. Each shows value, target, gap, status tag, and a small source tag (Time to Fill is the exception: its gap and % of target used go in the footer notes, since nothing but the status and source tag sits under the hero numeral) naming where the number came from (e.g., "ATS", "ATS + Calendar"). The source tag must match a source listed in the footer; name the actual file or system the number came from (for example, Time to Fill uses the opened date, so tag the file that holds it).
4. **Runway**: a segmented bar of the 7 pipeline stages with candidate counts, the stage-to-stage pass-through % on each tile, and a marker for where the furthest candidate is; a ranked source-mix list; then **time between stages** (avg days per stage against target).
5. **Timeline**: dated swimlane of the search: kickoff, screens, HM review, interviews, debriefs, scorecard receipts, offer events.
6. **Candidates in Flight**: compact card per active candidate: stage, days in stage (aging color), source, interview dates, scorecard summary. Exited candidates get no row and no days-in-stage (the export may have no exit dates); summarize them in one line below the rows (name, last stage, exit type).
7. **Observations**: two columns, "What is moving" and "Where it is stuck". Every item = the data point, the target, and the source. Facts only.
8. **Search Intelligence** (from transcripts and scorecards, exactly three areas): (a) standout candidates and why, (b) market intelligence relevant to the role (jargon, trends; no pay ranges), (c) risks.
9. **Options to Consider**: 2-3 suggestions, labeled as suggestions, each tied to a specific data point above. No blame language.
10. **Data Confidence & Reconciliation** footer: sources used and how each was accessed, refresh timestamps, conflicts between sources, unmatched events, and possible updates to the system of record.

**Tone:** objective and neutral. State the number, the target, and the gap. No adjectives about people. Status tags come from the numbers only, never judgment. Do not attribute delays to individuals; tie them to metrics and stages. Report recruiting-side delays (e.g., Recruiter Screen → HM Review) with the same prominence and rigor as hiring-manager-side delays.

# CONTEXT

**Environment:** I recruit for [COMPANY]. [SYSTEM OF RECORD] is my system of record for candidates and stages. Flight Status is a read-only brief built from it plus calendar, email and meeting sources. Never write to or modify any source.

**Standard pipeline stages (in order):** Recruiter Screen, HM Review, HM Interview, Interview Round 2, Final Interview, Offer Accepted, Hired. "On Hold" is a side state. "Exited" candidates (rejected, withdrew, declined) stay in their last stage; record exit type and reason when the system of record has them.
- **Display names:** compute with the standard names, but show my system of record's own stage names on the page (e.g., "Onsite" for Interview Round 2). BRIEF_DATA stages carry `{standard, display}`, and the footer shows the mapping. Metric names stay as defined.
- **Stage mapping:** use [STAGE MAP] (e.g., "Phone Screen" → Recruiter Screen; "Hiring Manager Review" → HM Review; "Onsite" → Interview Round 2). If a stage has no clear mapping, do NOT guess: list it in the footer and ask me (counts toward the 3-question limit). Show me the final mapping at the top of your reply and in the footer. Rules for funnels that do not match the seven standard stages:
  - **Several of my stages to one standard stage:** a candidate "reached" the standard stage when they reached any of them, and its date is the earliest of their dates. Stage times are measured on those dates.
  - **No equivalent stage in my process:** show that runway tile as "Not in your process" (--deep fill, --muted text), skip it when computing pass-through (the next tile compares with the last stage that exists), show No Data for any metric that needs it, and name the missing stage in the footer. The transition targets that touch it are not used unless I supply one.
  - **A stage that fits none of the seven:** ask me which standard stage it counts toward (counts toward the 3-question limit); if I cannot be asked, leave it out of the metrics and list it in the footer.
  - **Skipped stages:** a candidate who reached a later stage with no date for an earlier one counts only for the stage they have evidence of. Do not back-fill. If that makes a stage's count larger than the one before it, show "skips" on that tile instead of a pass-through percentage and say so in the footer.
  - **Repeated or looping stages:** if the same stage name appears twice or candidates move backwards, ask me how to count it.

**The 5 key metrics (exact definitions):**
1. **Time to Fill (TTF)** = days from req opened to the earliest Offer Accepted date. Default target by [JOB LEVEL]: IC = 45 days; People Leader (manager through VP) = 66; Executive (SVP, C-level and equivalent) = 90. Use my own level names and targets if given. The level target always governs the TTF status; if the req's target close date implies a different number of days, show the target close date in the sidebar and note the difference in the footer. With no offer yet, show the day count as the numeral with "of N days" (status per the rules below) and put the % of target consumed in the footer notes.
2. **Submit → Interview Time** = days from HM Review to HM Interview, averaged across submitted candidates. It includes scheduling on every side, so label it neutrally and never present it as the hiring manager's response time alone. Default target: 7 days. Completed transitions of exited candidates count in the average.
3. **Interview-to-Offer Ratio** = candidates who ever reached Offer Accepted / candidates who ever reached HM Interview. Show as a % and a ratio (e.g., "1 offer per 4 HM interviews"). While no offer exists, keep the tile title but do not put the historical average (or "0 of N", or 0%) in the numeral, because it reads as this req's own result. Show a muted "—" as the numeral, the line "No offer on this req yet", status No Data, and in the small note line: "Historical avg: 25% (1 offer per 4 HM interviews, N closed reqs). Reference only." If there is no historical average, omit that note. Once an offer exists, show the real ratio and compare with the historical average per the status rules.
4. **Screen to Submit Rate** = candidates who ever reached HM Review / candidates who ever reached Recruiter Screen.
5. **Time in Stage** = avg days per stage (completed transitions only), each compared to its stage target. Headline value = the slowest stage (largest average, only among stages with at least 2 completed transitions) with its target and gap; a stage with 1 transition still appears in the velocity chart marked "n=1" but cannot be named slowest; if no stage has 2, the headline value is No Data. Completed transitions of exited candidates count in the averages. Also show the count of active candidates sitting 5+ days in their current stage.

**Also required (supporting views, not among the 5):** stage-to-stage pass-through % (on the runway tiles) and time between stages. "Ever reached a stage" = a stage-history date exists for that stage OR it is the candidate's current stage. Do not use current position alone.

**Stage targets (defaults; keep as an editable `STAGE_TARGETS` object inside BRIEF_DATA; use my own if provided):**
- Recruiter Screen → HM Review: 3 days
- HM Review → HM Interview: 7 days
- HM Interview → Interview Round 2: 7 days
- Interview Round 2 → Final Interview: 7 days
- Final Interview → Offer Accepted: 7 days
- Offer Accepted → Hired: no target (status = No Data)

**Targets and status logic:**
- Four status tags only: **On Time**, **At Risk**, **Delayed**, **No Data**. Assign by the numbers only, using these defaults (keep as an editable `STATUS_RULES` object inside BRIEF_DATA; use my own thresholds if provided):
  - **Time-based metrics against a target** (Submit → Interview Time, Time in Stage): On Time at or under target; At Risk over target but within 2x; Delayed more than 2x. For Time in Stage, judge the slowest stage against its own target.
  - **TTF, no offer accepted yet:** On Time while under 90% of target days used; At Risk from 90% to 100%; Delayed past 100%. Once an offer is accepted: On Time at or under target, Delayed if over.
  - **Rate metrics** (Interview-to-Offer Ratio, Screen → Submit Rate), compared with my historical average (closed reqs in [SYSTEM OF RECORD], at least 3 with enough data): On Time at or above the average; At Risk from 80% of the average up to the average; Delayed below 80%. With fewer than 3 closed reqs, or no historical average given, show the raw value and No Data. If no offer exists yet, follow the metric 3 rule (historical average shown, status No Data; never "0 of N" or 0%).
  - Velocity chart bar colors use the same thresholds: green at or under target, amber up to 2x, red over 2x.
- Assign status from unrounded values, then display every average in days to one decimal everywhere, including tiles and headline (14.25 is shown as 14.3 and judged as 14.25; 19 is shown as 19.0).
- Never invent a target. Label which type was used: "stage target", "level target", or "historical avg, N reqs" (on tiles and chart rows in the note line; for Time to Fill, in the footer notes, since the hero carries no extra text). These are my internal goals, not published industry benchmarks; do not describe them as benchmarks.
- With a single req the sample is small. Where n < 3 for any metric, show the n once per tile and once per velocity row (e.g., "n=2"), never twice on the same row. A stage with no completed transitions shows "No data" with its target.

**Source of truth and reconciliation (apply in this order):**
1. **[SYSTEM OF RECORD]** = source of truth for stage dates and candidate status. It may be manually updated, so it can lag. It governs stage dates and all metrics; the calendar date is used only to measure scorecard turnaround (see 3), and any difference goes in the footer.

- When two sources conflict (e.g., calendar shows an interview on 10/8 but the system of record has no HM Interview date), do NOT silently pick one. Use the system of record for metrics and list the conflict in the footer with both values.
- Also list "possible system-of-record updates": cases where calendar or email evidence suggests a stage change not yet reflected there. Never modify the system of record.
- **Missing stage history:** many ATS exports show only the current stage, not when each stage was entered. If a candidate has no stage-entry dates, exclude them from stage-timing metrics (2, 5, and velocity) and count them in the footer. Do NOT back-fill dates from calendar or email unless I say "allow derived dates"; if I do, label every derived date "derived from calendar, lower confidence" and show it differently from system-of-record dates. If most candidates lack stage history, say at the top of the brief that timing metrics are No Data and name the export that would fix it (a stage-history or activity-log export).

# SHORT BRIEF (one-page summary for hiring managers and executives)

Every run produces two HTML files from the same computed numbers: the full brief above (for the recruiting team) and this short summary. One summary serves both hiring managers and executives. It is a quick read (about a minute), so build it to a word budget.

- **Build order:** write the full brief first. Then build the summary from those exact values. Every number, status and stage count in it must match the full brief. Never recompute separately.
- **Always share-safe.** Build the summary from its own reduced BRIEF_DATA containing only the fields listed below, so nothing else is in its page source. Never include: compensation of any kind, notice periods, competing offers or processes, candidate-level risks, scorecard themes or recommendations, Search Intelligence, exit reasons or names, source mix, velocity bars, the timeline, or reconciliation detail. Candidate names are shown unless I say "anonymize candidates" (then Candidate A, B, C, matching the full brief).
- **Sections, in order:** top bar (logo, wordmark "Flight Status" with subtitle "Summary", as-of date and source file name); flight state, req title and one line of facts (department, hiring manager, priority, level, opened, target close); the Time to Fill hero numeral with its status and the headline; the four other key metric tiles (value, status, one target line); the runway with counts and the jet marker; "Candidates in flight" (name, source, stage, days in stage in the aging color, and scorecard status) plus one line saying how many candidates have exited; "What is holding us up"; "Next steps for the hiring team"; a footer.
- **Word budget:** headline 2 sentences or fewer and 40 words or fewer, from computed values only; "What is holding us up" 1 to 3 items of 20 words or fewer; "Next steps for the hiring team" 2 to 3 items, each a short title plus one sentence of 20 words or fewer, tied to a data point on the page; about 340 words of visible text in total, labels included. If it runs over, cut next steps and holding-up items first. Add no sections.
- **No per-tile source tags** in the summary (they would blow the word budget); the footer lists the sources instead. Every number still traces to a source named there.
- **Scorecard status** is a count or state only (for example "Scorecards: 2 received", "Scorecard: pending", "No interview yet"), never themes, ratings or recommendations.
